Rules in force

  • Pins, not next door. Walkers drop a pin on each home as they go and find their own way on the map.
  • During a walk, every conversation belongs to a house. The yellow + is for conversations away from a door.
  • No conversation guides anywhere, and no email in forms. Contact is a cell phone with Text or Call.
  • Type: Schibsted Grotesk for names, titles and addresses; IBM Plex Mono for times, counts and dates.
  • One offset card per screen, on the one thing to do next. Everything else is grouped lists.
  • No floating button stacks. The map keeps only a locate button; other actions live in the top bar or a ⋯ menu.
  • The + always opens the logger, starting on the live walk when there is one (PL1).
  • Leaders draw or pick each route by hand, one at a time; no automatic splitting (PW2).
  • Conversations away from doors is a row at the top of People (PE1). Member invites are phone only, sent by text (AD3).
  • The walk page keeps its routes list, Edit teams and Team activity, but no mini map (WK3).
  • Long-press a house to drop a pin straight to Don't knock. A new pin with nothing logged disappears when you tap away (WM2).
  • Pin sheets: the outcome grid, then one quiet grey bar (Don't knock · Another home · History) on pins that already exist (WM3-B). To move a pin, tap away and tap the right house.
  • History everywhere uses the follow-up history component: date over time in mono on the left, the entry on the right (FU1, MP10, PE4).
  • Menus replace their sheet: when a ⋯ menu opens, the sheet under it steps away, and comes back on Close (MP12).

What informed the pin screens

  • Apple Maps · marking a location: the pin drops first, then a sheet shows the address, with Move as a main action.

  • Tide Guide · station map: one small dismissible hint teaches the gesture.
  • Uber Eats and Strava maps: search and chips float over a full-bleed map.
