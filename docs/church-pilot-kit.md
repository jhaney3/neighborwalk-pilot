# Church pilot kit

This is an **operator/leader preparation kit**, not an open enrollment offer or evidence of completed church pilots. For an iOS pilot, complete the current [iOS release gates](ios-release.md) before activation. The product owner must supply the actual operator, support commitment, policy approvals, hosting/provider decisions and commercial terms.

## The focused offer

**Turn neighborhood conversations into personal follow-through.** NeighborWalk helps a church prepare an outing, remember a respectfully recorded encounter, and give each requested next step an accountable person and date. The same workflow can serve neighborhood visits, community meals, service projects and referrals. Maps and optional conversation guides support that job; they are not the central promise.

The initial buyer is the leader responsible for outreach or local ministry. The everyday user is a volunteer who should be able to find their assignment, record the minimum useful detail and know what to do next. NeighborWalk complements a church's existing management system; it does not replace giving, attendance, safeguarding case management or a pastoral-record system.

Lead a demonstration with a missed-promise problem, not a map tour:

1. Plan a small outing and identify who is responsible.
2. Record an encounter, without inventing a person or address if neither is needed.
3. Give a requested next step a person and date.
4. Request a handoff; demonstrate that the original owner remains responsible until acceptance.
5. Complete or explicitly cancel the step, retaining the history and the neighbor's contact preferences.

Use the separate fictional demo. Do not include real neighbor records in sales screenshots, support tickets, training recordings or testimonials. Do not advertise app-store apps, national property coverage, unlimited offline use, guaranteed background sync/delivery, validated pricing or spiritual-outcome scores.

## Design-partner selection and agreement

Recruit 3–5 churches only through owner-approved contacts. Start with one accountable leader, a small adult volunteer group, a defined local ministry activity, supported phones and a willingness to report difficulties. Avoid high-risk counseling, safeguarding, children's records or highly sensitive service populations in this first field pilot.

Before onboarding, agree:

- The ministry workflow being tested, supported devices, initial geography and activities that are outside scope.
- Who may invite volunteers, publish guides, review exceptions and handle restricted information.
- What is permissible to record, how a neighbor can request a correction/no contact, retention/erasure responsibilities and who answers privacy requests.
- A support contact, service hours, incident escalation and the limits of after-hours support. Do not promise 24/7 coverage without staffing it.
- The observation period, feedback schedule, exit/export process and any actual pilot fee. Pricing is an experiment to be approved, not a validated subscription hidden in this document.
- Permission for narrowly scoped, content-free operational measurements. Participation is not blanket consent to publish church names, neighbor stories or screenshots.

## Operator preparation

1. Finish the live backup, isolated restoration, migration and provider gates. Verify an empty church can be provisioned with the first verified leader without sample records. A signed-in account alone does not grant membership.
2. Confirm the church name/timezone, named leader and escalation owner. Do not guess a leader from an existing email address or give one church access to another.
3. Verify first sign-in, invitation, expiry/wrong-account handling and access removal using authorized test accounts. Never ask a church to send passwords or recovery tokens to support.
4. Verify the real phones and optional map provider in the actual area. Demonstrate the address-list fallback and prepared-offline limits before anyone is in the field.
5. Record the exact deployed app/schema/policy versions, recovery point, support coverage and signed pilot terms. Keep them in the operator's private register, not this repository.

## Leader setup session — suggested 30 minutes

- Start in Today. Explain the owned-next-step promise and the unowned, overdue and unaccepted exceptions.
- Confirm church timezone, contact/recording rules and optional pathway preference. Keep pathway fields off unless the church has a clear, approved use for them.
- Invite the initial volunteers; verify the intended people accept with their own accounts.
- Create a reusable group and a small address list, or prepare an address-optional community activity. Do not assume a map contains every home.
- Create an outing with purpose, times, meeting point, leader contact and responsibility. Ask the assigned volunteer/group to acknowledge it before marking ready.
- Choose an optional outing guide. Review every prompt for respectful permission, the ability to decline and clear boundaries. Do not publish counseling instructions or unlicensed embedded content.
- Rehearse one fictional encounter and next step, including a handoff and no-contact request. Practice only in the separate demo or an explicitly isolated training workspace.
- Prepare each volunteer's real authorized workspace online, confirm what is cached, and rehearse safe sign-out/recovery. One live workspace tab per account/browser profile.

## Volunteer orientation — suggested 10 minutes

1. **Before leaving:** open Today, confirm your assignment, charge/lock your phone and prepare the app and guide while connected. Check the freshness message and current restrictions.
2. **At an encounter:** respect a declined conversation. Use a person record only when needed; an anonymous community encounter is a valid record. Do not invent an address or put private details in shared notes.
3. **When a next step is requested:** record what was requested, the responsible person and date. A group name alone is not individual accountability. A handoff is not complete until accepted.
4. **After saving:** distinguish “saved on this device” from “shared.” If a save fails, keep the form open. Do not clear storage, reinstall or switch accounts to fix unsent work.
5. **If something changes:** record restrictions explicitly. Ask a leader to review a mistaken encounter or duplicate. Do not reinterpret another volunteer's private notes.
6. **After the outing:** reconnect, confirm sharing, review your next steps and tell the leader about held conflicts or unaccepted handoffs. No app can replace checking in on a real promise.

## After each outing and weekly

The leader reviews outstanding owners, handoffs, overdue tasks, restrictions, device-recovery requests and factual corrections. Close the outing with a debrief that contains no private care notes. Do not count an entered-in-error encounter as activity or treat activity counts as spiritual outcomes.

The operator reviews availability/errors, provider delivery outcomes, recovery-point age, permission changes and support burden. Distinguish provider acceptance from delivery and a browser simulation from an actual-phone result. Exercise access removal and a restore drill on the agreed schedule; a recent backup timestamp without a restore result is not sufficient.

## Evaluation register — proposed measures, not completed results

Use an operator-controlled, access-limited weekly register. Keep denominators and exclusions explicit; small pilots do not establish population-wide performance.

| Question | Record without neighbor content | Decision it informs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can a leader start? | Minutes to first ready outing; assistance required | Onboarding friction |
| Can a volunteer use it? | Main-flow completion without coaching; device/browser family | Field usability |
| Do promises have owners? | Requested next steps with an owner/date; unaccepted handoffs | Core value |
| Are promises followed through? | Due tasks completed, explicitly cancelled or still open; explain cohort/date window | Follow-through, not spiritual impact |
| Is fieldwork trustworthy? | Duplicate/lost-write reports, held conflicts, recovery outcomes and resolution time | Release safety |
| Is support sustainable? | Tickets and support minutes per church/week, categorized without care content | Commercial viability |
| Would the church keep it? | Who would approve purchase, proposed budget/process, explicit willingness-to-pay response | Pricing and retention hypothesis |

Do not collect neighbor names, addresses, phone/email, note bodies, prayer/faith fields, precise volunteer movement, auth tokens or invitation links in product analytics. Church-level operational reporting is still sensitive and needs an agreed purpose, access and retention policy.

Ask after the first and final sessions: “What did you expect to happen?”, “Which requested next step would have been missed without this?”, “Where did you return to paper/text messages?”, “What would make you stop using it?”, and “Who would approve paying for this, and what would they need to see?” Record actual answers; do not turn a polite demo reaction into proof of market demand.

## Pilot exit and offboarding

Offer a deliberate, authorized export review; CSV exchange files and accessible-record JSON are not complete environment backups. Resolve open responsibilities and contact restrictions before removing access. Revoke memberships/sessions under the approved policy, preserve permitted outstanding authored work for supervised reconciliation, and confirm device/download handling with the church. Erasure of live, archived and backup records is a separate operator-controlled process, not a casual “clear data” action.

Record what must be retained, why, who can access it, when it expires, how a restore avoids reintroducing an erased record and how the church receives confirmation. Do not claim a complete erasure/offboarding implementation or verified tenant restoration until those procedures are implemented and rehearsed.

## Go, revise or stop

Continue toward a paid release only after repeated real use, no unresolved safety/data blockers, manageable support and credible purchase intent. Revise or cut features that distract from owned follow-through. Stop enrollment if records are lost, private information is exposed, restrictions are bypassed or support cannot safely handle recovery. Do not broaden into ChMS replacement, bulk messaging or additional geography to disguise a weak core workflow.
